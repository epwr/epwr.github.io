In unversity, I was taught that ~70% of jobs were never publically posted. I think within 2 years we'll see that number be greater than 90%.

# Why

I woke up today to a text from a friend. He's a software engineer who's looking for a new job, and had been playing around with an AI harness he sent me.

![A text message about automated job applications](/assets/imgs/text-re-ai-job-apps.png)

He's sending fully automated job applications. No human in the loop for selecting which jobs are a good fit, or reviewing the resume & coverletter. Job applications go out, he can decide if he's interested once he gets an interview.

I've hired for multiple positions and even before the ChatGPT moment we were getting 400+ job applications for many positions. Since then, the number has grown and I'm seeing >1000 direct applications per software engineer position. 

Along with the huge jump in applications, the signal to noise ratio is falling. 3 years ago, maybe 1 application in 20 got moved to an interview. Now it's closer to 1 in 50.

It seems reasonable to tie most of these applications to AI making it easier[^2]. If job applications are faster to research, write, and submit for job applicants then why wouldn't they apply to more jobs? Sure, there's other factors in play (eg. [tech jobs are having a rough moment](https://bsky.app/profile/josephpolitano.bsky.social/post/3mgfh6ddrxs26)), but not a >2x growth.

We've got a set of technical and cultural trends that drive lots of low effort job applications:
1. AI is quite good at tweaking resumes & writing coverletters, so it's easier to create a customized application.
2. With the recent surge of "claws"[^1], software that make it easy for non-technical users to run AI Agents, setting up automation to do job applications is getting much easier. 
3. Job seekers have been trained by companies to pump out lots of applications: many job applications disappear into a void, some companies post open jobs when they aren't hiring to try to trick investors & competitors they are growing, other companies hold open job applications to build up a backlog of resumes for when they do start hiring.
